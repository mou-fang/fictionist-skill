# Story Workflow

Use this reference for concept-to-chapter development, long outlines, story bibles, imported manuscripts, branch decisions, or when the user wants a complete fiction plan.

## 1. Brief Capture

Collect or infer:

- Genre and subgenre.
- Target reader and rating.
- Length: short story, chapter, novella, full novel, or serial.
- POV and tense.
- Tone: sweet, funny, dark, cinematic, lyrical, fast-paced, grounded, high-concept.
- Core promise: romance payoff, wonder, mystery, revenge, healing, survival, power growth, found family, or moral dilemma.
- Must-use facts and forbidden tropes.

Ask only when the missing answer would change the work dramatically. Otherwise label assumptions and continue.

For file-backed projects, create or update the workspace described in `project-workspace.md` before drafting. For one-off chat answers, keep the same thinking compactly in the response.

## 2. Premise Forge

Build the premise from four parts:

1. Hook: one specific unusual situation.
2. Pressure: why the protagonist must act now.
3. Desire: what the protagonist actively wants.
4. Cost: what they must risk, lose, confess, betray, learn, or become.

Reject premises where conflict exists only because people refuse a simple conversation, ignore obvious evidence, or act against their own interests without pressure.

## 3. Story Bible

For any sustained project, keep a compact bible:

- Logline and core promise.
- Theme stated as a dramatic question, not a slogan.
- Main cast with desire, fear, wound, mask, leverage, and contradiction.
- Relationship map: attraction, debt, rivalry, secret, power imbalance, trust level.
- World rules: what is possible, impossible, costly, taboo, rare, illegal, or misunderstood.
- Timeline: sequence, deadlines, travel time, recovery time, communication lag.
- Promise ledger: mysteries, emotional debts, planted objects, future reversals, relationship milestones.
- Continuity ledger: canon facts that must not drift.

Do not place unconfirmed possibilities in the canon ledger. Keep options in a decisions log or branch options file until the user chooses.

## 4. Outline Engine

For each act, arc, or chapter, specify:

- External event: what changes in the world.
- Internal movement: what changes in belief, desire, fear, or trust.
- Choice: what the protagonist decides under pressure.
- Consequence: what becomes harder because of that choice.
- Setup or payoff: what is planted, deepened, inverted, or resolved.
- Hook: what pulls the reader forward.

Avoid chapter lists that are only topics. Each beat should be causal: because X happens, the character chooses Y, which causes Z.

For each planned chapter, also track:

- Required canon facts to preserve.
- New facts expected to enter canon.
- Promises to plant or pay off.
- User decisions that constrain the chapter.

## 5. Scene Drafting

Before writing a scene, define:

- Scene goal: what the POV character wants right now.
- Friction: who or what blocks it.
- Subtext: what cannot be said directly.
- Turn: the irreversible change by the end.
- Emotional temperature: how the relationship or mood shifts.
- Exit hook: question, image, decision, danger, tenderness, or reveal.

Draft with concrete behavior. Let emotion appear through choices, attention, rhythm, sensory focus, and what characters avoid saying.

## 6. Revision Loop

After drafting or outlining:

- Remove contradiction before polishing language.
- Replace generic conflict with specific pressure.
- Add setup before payoff.
- Make coincidences cost something or turn them into choices.
- Strengthen chapter exits.
- Check that the emotional promise of the genre is being paid, not merely mentioned.

## 7. Branch And User Choice Handling

When the user wants to add a setting, choose a route, or change direction midstream:

- First identify what the request changes: canon, tone, relationship path, mystery answer, character arc, ending direction, or chapter pacing.
- Give non-spoiler impact notes: emotional effect, cost, risks, and which existing promises it strengthens or weakens.
- Ask focused questions before drafting when the answer matters.
- Keep options separate from canon until confirmed.
- After confirmation, update the story bible, canon ledger, timeline, outline, and decisions log before writing the next scene.

Good non-spoiler questions:

- "Should this make the relationship sweeter, more painful, or more complicated?"
- "Should this be a small personal secret or a plot-driving secret?"
- "Should the consequence appear immediately or become a later payoff?"
- "What must not change from the existing setup?"

## 8. Existing Manuscript Continuation

When continuing user-provided fiction:

- Read enough of the manuscript to understand voice, POV, tense, pacing, character dynamics, and last-scene pressure.
- Extract canon before writing: confirmed facts, timeline, rules, open promises, relationship state, and current stakes.
- Imitate the established voice unless asked to improve or change it.
- Preserve the user's original text and write continuation separately.
- If the manuscript contains contradictions, report them and propose fixes before continuing.
