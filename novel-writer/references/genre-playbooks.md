# Genre Playbooks

Use this reference when the user names a genre, asks for stronger genre flavor, or wants a story to be sweeter, more interesting, more suspenseful, more commercial, or more original.

## Romance Sweetness Engine

Sweetness comes from earned behavior under pressure:

- Private attention: one character remembers a tiny detail and acts on it later.
- Competence as care: a skill used for the other person's safety, dream, dignity, or secret.
- Chosen vulnerability: a guarded character risks honesty without being forced.
- Playful tension: banter, bets, fake rivalry, role reversal, or shared mischief.
- Mutual rescue: both people help each other in different ways.
- Boundary respect: desire grows because trust is protected.
- Public choice: one character chooses the other when it costs reputation, convenience, or control.

For fresh romance, change the relationship contract:

- Not only fake dating: fake rivals, fake exes, fake co-founders, fake enemies in public but secret allies.
- Not only boss and employee: equal experts with incompatible ethics, two caretakers of the same secret, competitors forced to protect one fragile person or place.
- Not only misunderstanding: a true conflict of values where both sides are partly right.

Quality gate: every sweet beat should also move plot, trust, or vulnerability forward.

## Sci-Fi Wonder Engine

A strong sci-fi idea needs:

- Speculative mechanism: the one impossible or future-facing thing.
- Rule: what it can and cannot do.
- Cost: energy, memory, identity, law, time, body, relationship, money, ecology, or truth.
- Social consequence: who profits, who suffers, who regulates, who rebels.
- Human stake: what intimate desire becomes impossible or newly possible.

Fresh sci-fi often comes from applying the idea to ordinary emotional life:

- Memory markets affect marriage, grief, education, crime, or inheritance.
- Time technology affects rent, parenting, medicine, labor, or apologies.
- Alien contact affects translation, cuisine, contracts, religion, dating, or disability.
- AI rights affect custody, mourning, authorship, citizenship, or class mobility.

Quality gate: the speculative premise must force choices that could not exist in a non-sci-fi story.

## Suspense And Mystery

Build suspense through fair pressure:

- The protagonist has a reason to investigate beyond curiosity.
- Clues are visible but misread because of believable bias.
- Red herrings reveal character or theme, not filler.
- Reveals reframe earlier scenes.
- Danger escalates because characters choose, conceal, trust, betray, or delay.
- The solution uses seeded facts, not late information.

Quality gate: the reader should feel surprised but able to look back and see the trail.

## Fantasy And Supernatural

Make power dramatic by limiting it:

- Define what power costs, what it cannot touch, and what taboo surrounds it.
- Show how rules shape jobs, laws, class, family, war, faith, or romance.
- Make magic or supernatural forces create moral debts.
- Seed the decisive rule before the climax.
- Let cultural details carry wonder: food, rituals, insults, clothing, architecture, funeral customs, contracts.

Quality gate: if the power disappeared, the society and plot should not remain basically identical.

## Comedy And Light Fiction

Comedy needs logic under absurdity:

- Give every funny character a serious private goal.
- Escalate from small social awkwardness to irreversible consequence.
- Use contrast: high-stakes language for tiny problems, ordinary language for wild problems.
- Avoid random jokes that stop the scene. Humor should expose desire, status, fear, or attraction.

Quality gate: the scene should still work as story if the jokes are softened.

## Web-Serial And Commercial Momentum

Use clear reader rewards:

- Immediate hook in the opening.
- Visible progress: skill, relationship, clue, status, territory, power, healing, or revenge.
- Delayed gratification with partial payoffs.
- Chapter-end pull: decision, reveal, emotional turn, threat, kiss-near-miss, clue, or reversal.
- Recurring engines: unresolved secret, ranking, mission, competition, contract, curse, countdown, debt, public performance.

Quality gate: each chapter should answer one question and open a stronger one.
